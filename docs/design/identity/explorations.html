fictty · identity · two directions

Every letter sits on a terminal grid. What fills the cells is the brand.

Both wordmarks are generated, not typeset. Each one is drawn from the same nine-row cell font and thrown fresh from a seed: no two throws are alike, and any throw can be kept. Thrown fills the cells with clay. Glass fills them with tinted panes that move like a gas.

Direction A

Thrown: wet clay that fires into cells

Each cell is a lump of clay, slumped into its neighbours, with gloss where it's wet and the odd thumbprint. Fire it and it dries through the potter's stages until it sets as crisp terminal cells. The mark is the agent's cursor, pressed from celadon-glazed clay.

      Direction B
      
Glass: clay if it were a gas

      
Every stroke is a pane of tinted glass, the way a terminal UI is panes stacked over panes. Where panes overlap, their tints mix into new colours. Throw it and the panes rush in from vapour and condense into letters in under half a second. After Larry Bell's coated-glass cubes and Josef Albers' overlapping squares: light, fast, and almost not there.

What it saysFast, light, layered, temporary. A UI is a stack of panes you can see through and rearrange.

      
Where it's strongOverlap is native to terminals (panes, layers, callouts) and to the product's idea of patching one layer at a time.

      
Where it's weakIt needs colour and transparency to read; in a plain 16-colour terminal it flattens to blocks.
