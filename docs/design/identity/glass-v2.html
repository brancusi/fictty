Identity · glass, second pass
01 · Wordmark

Every stroke is one pane of glass, slid into place on the cell grid.

The first pass scattered panes freely and tinted them at random. This pass gives the panes rules: tint by direction, move only along their own axis, in quarter cells. The word stays legible at any seed, and the seed still decides how it lands.

Day · multiply
Night · screen
seed -

The chosen form is seed -. Throw again replays the landing: panes slide along their own axis into their fixtures, letter by letter, in about half a second. Drag release to watch the word come apart without losing its rows.

Seeded variants

Six seeds from the same rules. They differ in which letters swap their tints, where the one warm pane lands, and which strokes sit a quarter cell off. Pick one to load it above.

How it's built

Nine rows of square modules (each one half a terminal cell, the ▀ of a half-block): two for ascenders, five for the x-height, two for descenders. Each glyph's runs of cells become panes; where two runs cross, the glass overlaps and the colour deepens by day and brightens at night. That crossing (in f, t, t) is the signature.

  • Tint by directionVertical strokes are cyan, horizontal ones violet, single cells amber. The seed may swap a whole letter, never one stroke.
  • Slide, don't scatterA pane moves only along its long axis, in steps of a quarter module, at most one step. Crossings always survive.
  • One warm lightThe i's dot is the warm pane. At most one other stroke turns amber or rose, chosen by the seed.
  • Small sizes go flatBelow eight pixels a module, drift, edges and glow switch off and the panes fill to 85%.

The mark

Three candidates on the same rules, each a few panes and their overlaps. The chosen one is a terminal's block cursor with a second pane laid over it: the cursor says terminal, the overlap says layers.

Small sizes

Day
Night

At 16 px the mark is two rectangles and one overlap, which survives the favicon grid. The wordmark holds down to about 88 px wide (three pixels a module); below that, use the mark.

02 · Colour

Daylight on a gallery wall, and tubes glowing in a dark room.

Glass colour is transmitted, so the two grounds mix it differently. By day, tints multiply: overlaps get deeper, like stacked filters. At night, tints add: overlaps get brighter, the edges carry the light, and colour spills onto the ground around each pane, the way Flavin's tubes colour the wall.

    04 · Rationale
    
    
    Light as a building material, in standard units.
    
    
    fictty makes interfaces that are assembled in a moment, reshaped while they run, and thrown away. The identity should behave the same way: made of interchangeable units, put together fast, held in place by a grid, and easy to take apart.
    
  
    

  
    
    
    Principles
    
    
      
      
    1. One moduleEverything sits on a square that is half a terminal cell. Panes snap to it; the only off-grid move is a quarter-module slide. The page uses the same module for spacing.
      2. 
      
    2. Light, not inkColour is transmitted, never painted. Tints multiply by day and add at night. Only type is opaque.
      3. 
      
    3. Units, not illustrationThe wordmark, the mark and the landing hero are arrangements of the same pane. Nothing is drawn that couldn't be taken apart and reused.
      4. 
      
    4. Symmetry holds the motionEvery move has a mirror or a centre. Staggers radiate out from the axis, so even a scatter reads as composed.
      5. 
      
    5. Fast in, calm hold, easy outPanes land in about half a second, hold still, and release without a fuss. Motion is for state changes only; nothing loops for decoration, and reduced-motion gets the resting state.
      6. 
    
    
  
    

  
    
    
    References, and what each gave
    
    
    
      
    Dan Flavin
    The standard tube
    Flavin used off-the-shelf fluorescent fixtures in a handful of stock colours. Taken: the pane as a stock industrial unit, a fixed small palette, and the night ground, where each pane's light falls onto the wall around it. His rows of vertical tubes became the hero's "in a row" state.
    
      
    Larry Bell
    Coated glass
    Bell's cubes are glass with vapour-deposited coatings that shift colour at the edges and go clear in the middle. Taken: panes as transparent volumes with no mass, and the iridescent edge: a thin cyan-violet-amber coat along each pane's long side.
    
      
    James Turrell
    Light as the material
    Turrell builds rooms where the light itself is the object and the aperture frames it. Taken: the night ground leans a little blue-violet instead of neutral black, and the terminal frame is treated as an aperture: colour is something you look into.
    
      
    Donald Judd
    Stacks and progressions
    Judd repeated identical units at equal intervals, and sized progressions by rule. Taken: the strict module, equal gaps, the symmetric height progression in the hero row, and the alphabet tiles that swap in mirrored pairs.
    
      
    Josef Albers
    Interaction of colour
    Kept from the first pass. Taken: the overlap is where the colour happens, so the crossings in f and t carry the identity.
    
    
    
  
    

  
    
    
    Rejected
    
    
      
      
    • Random tint per paneThe first pass looked lively but noisy, and no two throws felt like the same brand. Tint now follows stroke direction.
      • 
      
    • The oversized mark in the lockupThe large panes beside the word outweighed the letters. The mark is now its own compact symbol, used alone.
      • 
      
    • Scatter off the axisMoving panes sideways broke the letterforms at full scatter. Panes now slide only along their own length.
      • 
      
    • Frosted glass (backdrop blur)It reads as operating-system chrome, not light, and it hides the overlaps that make the colour. Panes stay clear.
      • 
      
    • Neon glow everywhereThe night glow is a soft wash on the ground and a bright edge, not a saturated bloom on type or UI.
      • 
      
    • Rotation, perspective, 3DOff the cell grid. A terminal can't do it, so the brand doesn't.
      • 
      
    • ClayHeavy and slow; it read as a craft shop. The name keeps the clay; the look moved on.
      • 
    
    
  
    

  
    
    
    Open choices
    
    
      
      
    • The headline. "Terminal screens agents write, patch and read back" is distilled from the pitch in the scope (write as one value, edit by id, read back exactly), which runs in full as the sub-head. Alternatives: "One value in, one screen out, read back exactly" or the README's "A terminal UI that is one value".
      • 
      
    • The mark. Cursor (chosen), crossing or stack. The crossing is the more distinctive shape but reads as a plus sign at 16 px.
      • 
      
    • Night as the default. The night ground shows the glass best and most terminals are dark. The site could default to night and follow the system only when asked.
      • 
      
    • Hero idle cycle. Assembled, in a row, as a screen, about every five seconds. It could run once on load and then wait for a click.
      • 
      
    • Type. Spline Sans and Spline Sans Mono, kept from the first pass for continuity. A narrower grotesk would push the industrial side further.
